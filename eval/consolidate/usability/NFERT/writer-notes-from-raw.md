# Notes on writing the NFERT piece

## 1. Wanted or needed, and not in the material

- **Scale.** How much synthetic nitrogen the world makes each year (Mt N), and what share of global energy use ammonia takes. The piece gives intensities and emission shares but no absolute tonnage or energy share, so the reader can't picture the size of the industry.
- **Where field N₂O comes from.** The material says field emissions are the larger life-cycle term (58.6% of the supply chain; two-thirds of the life cycle) but never says what produces them, or how much of that term is N₂O rather than CO₂ from, say, urea or liming. A reader outside the field will want the mechanism, and the piece's closing question depends on it. I left it out because the material doesn't give it.
- **The efficiency question.** The integration note at the top says the claim that matters is about nitrogen-use efficiency: whether the microbial community controls how much applied N is lost to leaching, denitrification and N₂O. The review contains no evidence on that. The piece can only raise it as an open question. Gao and Cabrera Serrenho's statement that NUE is the most effective lever is the only bridge, and it says nothing about microbes.
- **Legume savings in fertiliser terms.** Anglade et al. give a fixation share (87%) and a nitrogen *surplus* (21 vs 40–100 kg N/ha/yr), but not the fertiliser rate avoided, the yield, or the rotation's length or crops. "Matched conventional productivity" is unquantified. A surplus is not an input, so the headline saving can't be stated in kg N/ha.
- **Magnitudes for the weak channel.** "Modest" free-living fixation has no number (kg N/ha/yr). Mineralisation is given as 1–4% of soil organic N per season with no soil N stock, so there's no kg N/ha figure to set against crop demand.
- **What the inoculants were.** Schütz et al. (2018) is reported only as yield gains by climate. The material doesn't say which organisms (N-fixers, mycorrhizae, phosphate solubilisers), and the paper's title promises nutrient-use-efficiency results that the material doesn't report.
- **The Ladha suppression example.** "One system" with no crop, location or baseline fixation rate.
- **Low-carbon routes.** Tunå et al. is titled "nonfossil ammonia production" and Razon is an LCA of cyanobacterial ammonia, but the material reports only their fossil-route figures. Electrolytic hydrogen and the cyanobacteria route would have been natural to mention, but the material has nothing on them beyond the titles. Carbon capture (0.79 t CO₂/t NH₃) is the only abatement figure given.
- **Gao's mitigation result.** The title says emissions "could be reduced by up to one-fifth of current levels by 2050". The body doesn't report what that means, and the wording reads two ways ("by one-fifth" or "to one-fifth"), so I didn't use it.

## 2. Hard to use

- **The integration note at the top** is project-internal: "¶36", "the proposal", a re-scoped brief, Refs.bib. It overrides the review's own framing ("Q2 answered the wrong question") without adding evidence, and a standalone reader has no access to the proposal it refers to. I took one thing from it, the substitution-versus-efficiency distinction, and stated it as an open question rather than a finding.
- **Mixed units and gases.** CO₂ and CO₂-equivalent are used side by side (1.6 t CO₂; 1.92 t CO₂e; 2.8 t CO₂), and then turned into a single per-N range "1.9–3.4 t CO₂e". The range comes from multiplying 1.6 and 2.8 t CO₂ by 1.21, so it is really a CO₂ range labelled CO₂e. In the piece I kept the source's label.
- **Energy figures on different bases.** 28 / 37 / 59 GJ per tonne (process or plant energy?) sit next to ecoinvent's 41 GJ of *primary* energy. The material doesn't reconcile them. The "global average ~37 GJ" has no explicit citation (it comes in a sentence whose neighbours cite Tock, Demirhan and Tunå).
- **Comparing the share figures.** The three emission shares use different years (2007 IFA vs 2018 Menegat; Gao's year not given) and different boundaries. The manufacture share combines a 2007 association figure with process studies of unstated year.
- **The implied life-cycle intensity.** Manure at 23 t CO₂e/t N being "1.9 times an average synthetic fertiliser" implies about 12 t CO₂e/t N for synthetic N over its life cycle. I used this, attributed to Gao. One-third of ~12 is ~4 t CO₂e/t N for production, which sits above the 1.9–3.4 manufacture range from the other studies. Whether that's a boundary difference (e.g. Gao counting production of all N fertilisers) or a real disagreement can't be told from the material.
- **Stoichiometric conversions.** The per-N figures are derived, which the source flags. That's honest, but it means the headline per-N numbers trace to no single paper.
- **Rhetorical padding.** The Hume billiard-ball aside and the repeated INDIRECT labels carry the review's reasoning but not data. The attribution point they make is real and is in the piece; the framing is not.
- **Search provenance.** The Scholar Gateway line (12 passages, 11 articles) doesn't match the 10 references listed, and the agronomy sources came from "general web search". This doesn't affect the piece, but it makes the evidence base hard to audit from the material alone.
