# Notes on writing the piece

## 1. Wanted or needed, not found in the material

- **A stated rationale for why function might be more predictable.** The opening frames the question with the functional-redundancy argument (many taxa can do the same job, so environment selects for the job). The material implies it (Louca's "decoupling", Burke's 15% vs 70%) but never states it as the field's expectation, and its "Do not say" list warns against framing evidence against "a hypothesis the literature reviewed does not state". I wrote it as a tempting guess rather than as what ecologists believe.
- **What NEON is.** The material says only "the independent NEON survey across the continental USA". I did not expand the acronym or say what NEON samples.
- **A plain explanation of the two R² measures.** The material names "R² of the best-fit line" and "R² against the 1:1 line" but does not explain the difference. My one-line gloss (tracks the rise and fall versus hits the values) is general statistics, not from the material.
- **Why the 1:1 skill collapses.** The material says only that the authors "link bias partly to compositional data". I would have liked a sentence on the mechanism for a lay reader and left it out.
- **What Liu's neutral-model migration rate means.** The material says a high neutral-model migration rate lowers held-out skill, with no explanation of the model or why. I cut the point; explaining it would have needed outside knowledge.
- **Effect sizes for the abundance effect.** Abundance is the best-supported property ("Moderate", three microbial studies), but no numbers are given, so the piece can only state the direction.
- **Werbin's 91% and 50% accuracy.** Quoted from the abstract with no saying what they measure. Unusable.
- **Averill's main text and Extended Data Fig. 1** (a within-NEON random cross-validation of functional against taxonomic groups). Either might reconcile the authors' "fungal predictability rises with taxonomic scale" with the non-monotonic supplementary medians. Without it I had to leave that tension open ("perhaps from a different summary").
- **Hold-out designs of the two 2026 reports** (Aouabed; the New Phytologist canopy-reflectance study, whose authors are not given, so I could only cite it by journal and DOI).
- **Any measured-gene evidence beyond Faure 2021**, any functional-hierarchy evidence (gene to pathway to category), any spatially blocked microbial test, and any test scored against a mean-profile or persistence baseline for both profile types. The material says these were not found or not searched, which is the piece's main finding, but it means the "resolution" question gets a one-sided answer.
- **Numbers for Werbin's rank results and for the 2026 reflectance study by rank.** Only the direction is given (validation R² 0.27 to 0.86 "varying with taxonomic resolution").

## 2. Hard to use in the material

- **Reconstructing study designs from scattered claims.** Each study's design, numbers and caveats are spread across several sections (Averill appears under at least five headings). I had to assemble them before writing.
- **The "Say instead" wordings are long and caveat-heavy.** They are accurate but read as audit text; turning them into prose for an outsider while keeping every qualifier was the main effort, and some qualifiers (for example "verified in the abstract by the critic", "limited by access") I dropped as not meaningful to a reader outside the pipeline.
- **Two duplicated or slightly inconsistent figures.** Bacterial phylum median is "0.23 to 0.30" in one place and "0.27" in another (explained by duplicate-row handling, but only in one bullet). Liu's functional range appears as both "32.6 to 56.8%" and "33 to 57%".
- **Mixed measures across studies** (best-fit R², 1:1 R², correlation, nRMSE, CRPS, AUC, Bray-Curtis similarity). The material says correctly that they cannot be compared, but it leaves the writer with numbers that cannot be placed side by side, so I used few of them.
- **Evidence standing is hard to convey compactly.** The material distinguishes searched-and-not-found, not searched, and not stated whether searched. For a lay reader I collapsed these to "no study was found", which slightly overstates how thorough the search was for some gaps (for example the functional hierarchy, which was not searched at all).
- **The freshwater explanatory pattern** is rated "Moderate and replicated" by the research and "moderate at best, not replicated" by the second reader. I followed the second reader.
- **Werbin's specifics are second-hand within the pipeline** (65% versus 8% CRPS, calibration-site evaluation), yet they carry much of the "what makes prediction fail" section. I flagged the preprint status but not this.
- **A self-contradiction on access** flagged in the Gaps section (sources listed as "not obtained" are cited as read in abstract) made it unclear how far to trust some explanatory citations (Louca, Galand, Bruelheide).
