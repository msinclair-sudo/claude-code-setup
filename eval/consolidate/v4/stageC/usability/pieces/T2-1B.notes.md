# Notes

## 1. Wanted or needed, not in the material

- **Why the nodes are featureless.** The material never says what a ghost is in a citation graph. I guessed it is a work that is linked to (cited) but has no text embedding in the pipeline, and wrote around it ("nodes that have no vector") rather than state the guess.
- **What the degree threshold is.** "Degree >= 2" is used throughout but never defined. I wrote "keeping only nodes with at least two links" on the assumption that it filters nodes by link count; whether it counts in-links, out-links or both is not given.
- **Which direction the bridge runs.** The material calls A -> ghost -> B a "co-citation bridge" but also describes it as two documents sharing a cited third work, which is bibliographic coupling, not co-citation. I avoided both terms and described the structure.
- **How ghosts are actually anchored in the pipeline now.** The material discusses an imputed anchor ("the diffusion average") and a zero anchor, but does not say which the pipeline currently uses, so the piece treats both as options.
- **Any numbers on the pipeline itself.** No measurement of how close A and B end up, how many bridges survive to the clusters, or what share of ghosts a threshold of 3 would leave. The review recommends these measurements but none exist, so the piece can only say "not tried".
- **The reasoning behind the 1,405/3,392 split.** I had the count for one example graph ("fallworm") but nothing on whether 70% is typical across graphs.
- **What the "standard benchmark" in Morehead et al. is.** The material names Cora and a GCN; I kept it generic because Cora means nothing to an outside reader and there was no description of it.
- **What the review's search covered.** For most claims the material says it does not know whether contrary evidence was searched for. Only the missing head-to-head test of ghost designs is marked as searched for and not found, so that is the only absence the piece states as a search result.
- **What "dense, low-dimensional" features meant in Ferrini et al.** SPECTER2 is 768-d, which is not obviously low-dimensional, so the transfer of that finding to SPECTER2 is the review's reasoning, not something the material shows directly.

## 2. Hard to use in the material

- **Organised around a project, not the topic.** Everything is framed for "biblion" and its four internal questions (D1-D4), all four of which show "No answer to this question in the research" in the per-question slots, while the actual answers sit in an untied TL;DR and key-findings block. I had to rebuild the three questions of the brief from the concept sections.
- **Morehead et al. contradiction.** The material says PCA-100 "reduces prediction variance across seeds", but its own figures (std 0.62 original, 0.80 with PCA) show the opposite. The Gaps section flags this. I used only the accuracy figures and dropped the variance claim.
- **Reviewer reasoning mixed with findings.** Many bullets are "the reviewer's reasoning" or "judgement" rather than sourced results, and they sit beside proven results in the same lists. Keeping that line visible to a lay reader took deliberate wording ("the review judges", "the review infers", "that is reasoning, not a result").
- **Unattributed or URL-only sources.** Several claims rest on "Anonymous (n.d.)" URL entries (the APPNP closed form, the SVD-ordering point, part of the PCA-with-missing-data point), "deep bibliographic coupling work" has no reference, and the masked-aggregation claim names no paper. I either cited the named source that shared the claim (e.g. Josse and Husson) or left the claim uncited as general mechanism; I dropped the impute-then-SVD vs SVD-on-observed comparison because its only source is an anonymous URL.
- **Jargon left unexplained.** MCAR, homophily, per-channel masking, teleport term, Dirichlet energy, mutual reachability, second-order proximity: the material assumes the reader knows them. Only MCAR has a background definition.
- **Repetition.** The same claims recur in the TL;DR, key findings, concept sections and "Do not say", sometimes with slightly different wording and attribution (the bridge claim is attributed to Tang et al., to Um et al. and to "the reviewer's synthesis" in different places), which made it harder to tell which source supports what.
- **Standing labels are uneven.** A few claims carry ratings ("well supported", "PARTIALLY DEMONSTRATED"), most carry none, and "Evidence: derived" is not explained. I could not use these to grade claims consistently.
