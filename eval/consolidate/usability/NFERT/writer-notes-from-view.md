# Notes on writing the NFERT piece

## 1. Wanted or needed, and not in the material

- **Scale.** No figure for how much synthetic nitrogen (or ammonia) the world makes or applies per year, and nothing on how much food depends on it. A reader outside the field needs this to judge whether 1–2% of global emissions is a lot. The 0.47 Gt and 1.13 Gt totals are there, but not the tonnage behind them.
- **Field N₂O itself.** The material says field emissions are the larger term (58.6% of the supply chain) but gives no emission factor, no N₂O per tonne of N applied, and no split of "field emissions" into N₂O versus other gases (CO₂ from urea, for example). I could not say what "field emissions" consists of.
- **The implied synthetic life-cycle figure.** Manure at 23 t CO₂e/t N is "1.9 times an average synthetic fertiliser", which implies about 12 t CO₂e/t N for synthetic N over its life cycle. That is a useful contrast with the 1.9–3.4 t at the gate, but the material never states it, so I left it out rather than derive it.
- **Nitrogen-use efficiency.** Named as the single most effective lever, but not defined, and no typical values (what fraction of applied N crops actually take up). I supplied a plain-language definition inferred from the gap statement ("fraction of applied N that is taken up versus lost").
- **Decarbonisation routes beyond capture.** Only carbon capture (0.79 t CO₂/t NH₃) appears. Nothing on electrolytic ("green") hydrogen or other low-carbon routes, which a reader will expect in a piece about manufacturing emissions.
- **Context for the legume numbers.** The European organic rotation (Anglade et al. 2015): which country or region, which crops, and whether "matched conventional productivity" means yield per hectare or per rotation. The 81–86% suppression figure (Ladha et al. 2022): which legume, which setting.
- **Mechanism.** Why mineral N suppresses fixation; why so little of soil organic N mineralises. Both would help a non-specialist and neither is given.
- **Free-living fixation magnitude.** "Modest" with no number (kg N/ha/yr). Hard to make "rarely large enough to displace a defined fertiliser rate" concrete without it.
- **Inoculant content.** Which organisms (rhizobia, Azospirillum, phosphate solubilisers?) the 171-study meta-analysis covered, and whether the yield gains were N-related at all, given the stated dependence on soil phosphorus.
- **Reference list.** Only author-year citations; no titles or journals, so the piece cannot carry a usable reference list.
- **Energy basis.** Whether the GJ figures are primary energy, and whether they include feedstock energy. The ecoinvent figure is labelled "primary energy" (41 GJ), the others are not.

## 2. Hard to use in the material

- **The ecoinvent energy figure sits outside the summary range.** 41 GJ primary energy per tonne of ammonia lies above the stated 28–37 GJ range, with no note on whether the bases differ. I left the 41 GJ out of the piece.
- **CO₂ versus CO₂-equivalent.** Per-ammonia figures are mostly t CO₂; ecoinvent and the per-N range are t CO₂-equivalent. The material converts one into the other (1.6–2.8 t CO₂/t NH₃ to 1.9–3.4 t CO₂e/t N) without saying whether the gas basis changed. The arithmetic works only via the 1.21 conversion, so I treated them as the same basis, which may be wrong.
- **Manufacturing share: two overlapping figures.** "0.9% to 1.8%" combines the IFA 0.93% (2007) with "process studies at roughly 1% to 1.8%" from unnamed sources. Usable, but the process studies have no citation.
- **Do-not-say item 4 refers to things the material does not explain.** It mentions "the proposal" and "the integration read", and says the review "reported a null against a claim the proposal does not make". I could tell what to avoid (reading the substitution result as an answer on nitrogen-use efficiency) but not what the proposal is.
- **Standing markers are sparse.** Only two claims carry a bracket, both `(repl)`. Everything on microbes has no stated standing, though the Gaps section shows each rests on a single source. I had to infer from Gaps how firmly to hold them.
- **"No critic checked it."** Repeated on both answers and in Gaps. Clear enough, but it left me deciding how much to tell the reader; I closed the piece with a short provenance caution.
- **Repetition.** The same sentences recur up to three times (answer, concept bullets, do-not-say "say instead"). Not wrong, but it made it harder to see what was distinct; the verbatim "say instead" wording is also stiff and I paraphrased it.
- **Directional confounders.** "Reduced fertiliser need co-varies with organic matter, water and rotation" gives no direction (more organic matter, wetter soils?), so I kept it non-directional.
