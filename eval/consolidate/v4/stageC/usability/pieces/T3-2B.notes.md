# Notes on writing piece.md

## 1. Wanted or needed, not in the material

- **Why N₂O matters.** The material never says N₂O is a greenhouse gas or ozone-depleting, or gives any reason emissions matter (it only implies it, e.g. P460 "can potentially mitigate N2O emissions"). An outside reader needs this in the first paragraph. I left it out and framed N₂O only as the point where the chain can stop.
- **Why truncation happens in real denitrifiers.** Nothing on how common incomplete denitrifiers are, which conditions (pH, O₂, copper, carbon) cut off NosZ, or how much N₂O comes from denitrifiers versus nitrifiers. The only mechanisms named are the modelling ones (Cu limitation, regulatory lag, missing *nosZ*, electron competition).
- **What nitrifier denitrification and hydroxylamine oxidation are**, beyond the two reactions named. I explained it only as "using part of the pathway", which is thin for a reader outside the field.
- **What "exchange reaction" and "dynamic FBA" mean.** The material uses both without definition. I wrote the exchange-reaction explanation from the FBA definition (steady state, so an internal metabolite cannot accumulate), which is my inference from Orth et al. plus the reviewer's note that N-oxides "can be drained by exchange reactions". Dynamic FBA I left undefined.
- **Why the kinetic family succeeds**, stated directly. The material asserts that kinetic, cybernetic and trait-based models resolve stepwise N₂O but does not say what about rate equations makes accumulation natural. My sentence "each step can run at its own speed and N₂O builds up whenever its production outpaces its reduction" is my own gloss.
- **What kinetic and trait-based models give up** (parameter burden, lack of genome-scale detail). Without it the comparison is one-sided; the material has no weaknesses of those families at all.
- **Any quantitative result from the kinetic or trait-based models**: fit to data, predicted N₂O fractions, anything comparable to the 15% and 60% figures given for the FBA models.
- **A full citation for Pan et al.** (no year, authors or title), and for Domingo-Félez & Smets, Oberhardt et al. 2008, AGORA/AGORA2 and CarveMe. I cited Pan as incomplete and dropped the others.
- **What "17 M yield ratios" means.** The "M" is unexplained (molar?). I wrote "17 reported yield ratios".

## 2. Hard to use

- **Contradiction on the 2016 network.** One place says only Perez-Garcia 2014 and Mellbye 2018 emit N₂O as a distinct flux; the verdict and model index include Perez-Garcia 2016. I described the 2016 result (N₂O formation in simulation) without claiming it as one of "the only" models, and avoided any count.
- **Contradiction on what caused the 60% drop**: "NO oxidation to NO2(-)" in the body versus "CytP460 reduces N2O by 60%" in the model index. I used the direct quote (NO oxidation) and left cytochrome P460 out of that sentence.
- **The verdict paragraph is written to a hidden question** ("the user's mechanism", "the specific full target") whose wording is not given. It took some reconstructing to see that the question was specifically about N₂O as a truncation product in a four-step chain.
- **Validation conditions versus mechanism.** The 15% validation was under non-limiting O₂ and ammonium, while the "validated" central finding concerns O₂ limitation. The material does not say whether the overflow mechanism itself was tested against data. I reported both facts and did not draw a conclusion.
- **Several load-bearing claims have no source** (the four-step pathway itself, the 10-electron count, the biomass-objective limitation, the "error-prone" balancing claim, AGORA and CarveMe). I used the pathway and the biomass-objective point because the piece needs them, and dropped the rest.
- **Heavy repetition.** The same verdict appears four or five times in slightly different wordings, which made it hard to tell whether apparent differences (e.g. "only in N. europaea" versus "ammonia-oxidizing bacteria" generally) were meant.
- **Zakem 2024 cited twice** (bioRxiv and PNAS) for identical claims; I cited the PNAS version only.
- **"Cybernetic" models** are named as a family but never explained, so I could only place them next to kinetic models and say they blur the boundary.
