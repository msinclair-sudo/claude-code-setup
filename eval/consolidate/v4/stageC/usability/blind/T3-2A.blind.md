Errors (quote each sentence):
- "N₂O building up in a complete denitrifier has other causes: NosZ missing or inhibited, steps competing for electrons, regulation lagging, or the pathway split across organisms." A complete denitrifier by definition has NosZ. "Missing" applies to truncated denitrifiers, not to a complete one.
- "A 2016 follow-up widened the network ... and found that oxidising NO back to nitrite cut N₂O formation by 60% without changing how much nitrite the culture produced." This was a simulation result from a metabolic network model, but "the culture produced" makes it read as an experimental measurement.
- "Models that resolve all four steps, with N₂O as an intermediate that can accumulate, are mostly kinetic ... Kampschreur and colleagues (2012) modelled *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* ..." A. tumefaciens C58 lacks nosZ, and its model covers Nap, NirK and NorB (three steps), not four. Listing it here implies a four-step model.
- "Genome-scale models exist for several bacteria that carry all four steps: ... two models of *Pseudomonas stutzeri* A1501 and two of *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*." P. aeruginosa has more than two published genome-scale reconstructions (e.g. iMO1056, iPAU1129/iPAE1146 and later ones). The count presumably reflects what the search found, but it reads as a complete count.
- "And an FBA model that maximises biomass does not naturally predict that a cell will spill a by-product such as N₂O; that takes extra constraints." Overgeneralised. Biomass-maximising FBA does predict secretion of some by-products without added constraints (e.g. fermentation products under anoxia). The point holds for N₂O in a cell that has NosZ, but not for by-products in general.
- (Possible, minor) "commonly maximising biomass production or uptake of the energy substrate". Substrate uptake isn't a standard FBA objective; biomass or ATP yield is the usual alternative.

Notes:
- Oversimplified but not counted as an error: "Each reductase becomes a separate reaction balanced against a pool of electron donors (NADH or quinones)". Nir, Nor and Nos take electrons from cytochrome c and cupredoxins, not straight from NADH or quinones. A model can lump these, though.
