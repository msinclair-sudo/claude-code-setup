## dns.4 (do not say)

"The shortage of lime in sub-Saharan Africa shows that lime is a scarce resource."

- why: The constraint there is access, not resource: the Ethiopian case study locates the failure in the value chain, not in the deposits, and a regional access constraint on a locally abundant mineral is a distribution problem that should not be conflated with a scarcity problem.
- instead: liming-shortfall.5, liming-shortfall.7, liming-shortfall.8
- trail: CB1-LIMING §Verdict on the scarcity claim

From the note:

> CB1-LIMING:A-021 [Verdict on the scarcity claim] The broad one is *access*, not *resource*.
>
> CB1-LIMING:A-022 [Verdict on the scarcity claim] Lime is genuinely hard to obtain in parts of sub-Saharan Africa: Silva et al. state that "lime is not widely available in the region," with crop yield loss to soil acidity on 32.7 Mha, 23% of sub-Saharan Africa's cropland, and a burden of US$6.0 billion [14].
>
> CB1-LIMING:A-023 [Verdict on the scarcity claim] But the Ethiopian case study of that same problem locates the failure in the value chain, not in the deposits [8].
>
> CB1-LIMING:A-024 [Verdict on the scarcity claim] A regional access constraint on a locally abundant mineral is a distribution problem wearing the costume of a scarcity problem, and the two should not be conflated.
