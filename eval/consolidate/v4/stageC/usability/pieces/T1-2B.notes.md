# Notes on writing piece.md

## 1. Wanted or needed, not in the material

- **The central comparison itself.** A held-out comparison of measured metagenomic gene profiles against taxonomic profiles from the same samples. The material says none was found, so the piece's main question ends in "unknown". I could only describe what exists around the gap.
- **Any held-out evidence on functional resolution** (gene family vs pathway vs broad category). None at all; the nearest material is plant traits (Deschamps 2023) and an explanatory redundancy result (Chen 2022). The resolution question in the brief is therefore answered only for taxa.
- **Averill et al. 2021 main text.** The authors' "equally predictable" and "fungal predictability rises with taxonomic scale" claims rest on a summary in the unread main text (Fig. 4). The only numbers available are a reviewer's recomputation from the supplement, which disagrees with the authors in places. I couldn't say why they disagree.
- **Averill Extended Data Fig. 1** (within-NEON 70/30 cross-validation, functional vs taxonomic). The critic says it bears directly on the question, but no numbers are given. This would have been a useful contrast between a random within-survey split and a cross-survey transfer in one study.
- **Numbers for Werbin's functional-vs-taxonomic result.** Only stated qualitatively ("more predictable than most taxonomic groups"). I had to report it without magnitudes.
- **Aouabed et al. 2026 details.** Abstract only, hold-out design unverified. Only one number (fungal functional R² 0.45 on NEON), with no taxonomic counterpart, so I kept the citation to its qualitative claims.
- **The 2026 New Phytologist canopy-reflectance study** has no authors or title in the material, only a DOI and a few findings. I could only cite it by journal and DOI.
- **Plain definitions of the error measures** (CRPS, nRMSE). Werbin's 65% vs 8% figure is "CRPS worse by"; I rendered it as "forecast error rose by", which is right in direction but I couldn't explain the measure.
- **Basic study sizes and predictors for Averill** (number of training sites, which predictors), useful for an outsider to judge how hard the transfer was.
- **A typical skill level for macro-organism community models at new sites**, to give a reader a scale for whether R² 0.15 or 0.28 is poor or ordinary. Norberg 2019 gives only variance partitioning, not headline skill.
- **Any microbial study scoring either profile against a mean-profile or persistence baseline.** The material says none exists; the baseline argument therefore leans on one trait-only stream study (Statzner 2005), whose held-out status is itself second-hand.
- **Why bacterial derived functions transfer worse than fungal ones** (mechanism). Only Werbin's autocorrelation account, which concerns a temporal hold-out, not new sites.
- **The trait-based SDM transferability literature** (Vesk 2021, Pollock 2012, fourth-corner/HMSC) is named by the critic as the closest plant/animal counterpart but was never searched, so the "what makes it succeed or fail" section is thinner on trait-based models than it should be.
- **Measured-gene properties that might matter** (gene copy number, genome-size normalisation, annotation completeness, compositional closure) are listed as untested; I would have liked at least one result.

## 2. Hard to use

- **Two voices that disagree.** The review's answers and body, then a critic who corrects them in five places. I had to reconcile every load-bearing claim against the critic: Averill's "equally predictable" refers to high-level taxa only (so I gave the kingdom split against phyla); the bacterial phylum median is 0.23 to 0.30 in the table but 0.27 in the critic (I wrote "roughly 0.27"); "almost every group had no 1:1 skill" is a median statement, with about 21 of 130 groups above zero; Van Sickle 2005 is calibration-site precision, not held-out skill (I dropped its numbers); "trait profiles barely vary, so a constant predicts them" is reviewer inference contradicted by the Statzner abstract (I kept the baseline logic as a general point and did not attribute it to Statzner); Hawkins 2000's species-over-family result is not confirmed as held-out (I left it out).
- **Process metadata mixed into the content.** Search routes, preflights, ROUTING, access labels on every claim, Crossref author-list errors, a duplicated "Sources not obtained" list (several entries appear twice). Useful for judging reliability, but it had to be stripped for a lay reader.
- **Jargon and codes.** D1/D2/D3, Case A to D, O/E, Pc, LIRR, RIVPACS, CRPS, nRMSE, FAPROTAX, "derived" vs "measured" used as technical labels. Each needed translating or dropping.
- **Non-commensurable numbers.** Best-fit R², 1:1 R², correlation, CRPS change, AUC pass rates, Bray-Curtis similarity, SD of O/E. The material is clear these can't be set side by side, which makes it hard to give the reader any sense of scale without implying comparisons the evidence doesn't support.
- **Merged evidence in the freshwater "trait advantage".** The review folds together fit to environment, consistency across regions and lower variability. The critic separates them, and notes the abstract-only access and the single author network. I compressed this to one sentence and a caveat.
- **Overlapping authorship** between Averill 2021 and Werbin 2024 (same group, same NEON data) affects how independent the "three studies" are; the material doesn't flag it in the answers, and I didn't have room to raise it.
