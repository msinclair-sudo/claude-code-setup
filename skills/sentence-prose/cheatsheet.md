# Cheatsheet — Reader-Expectation Writing (Gopen & Swan)

## The sentence map
```
[ TOPIC POSITION ........... middle ........... STRESS POSITION ]
   old info, whose story        old+new          new info to emphasize
   "first things first"                          "save the best for last"
                    └── verb should arrive early, carrying the ACTION ──┘
```

## The seven principles (revision checklist)
| # | Principle | Quick check |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Subject → verb, close together | Count words between subject and its verb |
| 2 | New/emphatic info → stress position | What sits just before the period? Is it the point? |
| 3 | Story-owner → topic position | Whose story is this sentence? Are they up front? |
| 4 | Old/linking info → topic position | Does the opening connect backward? |
| 5 | Action → in the verb | Read the verbs alone — do they tell the story? |
| 6 | Context before new | Is the reader oriented before the new idea? |
| 7 | Structural emphasis = substantive emphasis | Does the structure stress what actually matters? |

## Decision rules
- **Sentence feels off, verb is far from subject** → if the gap matters, split with a semicolon; if it's an aside, delete it.
- **Two+ ideas both deserve emphasis** → add a colon/semicolon to open a second stress position (lead-in must stand alone). One closure = one stress slot.
- **Paragraph feels disconnected** → run the topic-string test; re-thread one old-information strand through the sentence openings.
- **Promised idea never paid off** → stress→topic hand-off broke; write the missing connecting sentence(s).
- **Many players, no clear happening** → run the verb test; surface the real action verbs, fix one topic-position actor.
- **Active or passive?** → choose whichever keeps the paragraph's subject in the topic position. Not a rule, a focus decision.

## "Too long" — the real definition
A sentence is too long when it has **more candidates for stress than stress positions available** — NOT past any word count. (The mythical 29-word limit is wrong; 10-word sentences can be impenetrable, 100-word sentences can flow.)

## Tells & smells
| If you see... | You're probably... |
|---|---|
| Long phrase between subject and verb | demoting real content to "interruption" (Ch 2) |
| Sentence ends on a trivial word | wasting the stress position (Ch 3) |
| Every sentence opens with a new subject | shifting focus; reader can't find the story (Ch 4) |
| Verbs are mostly *is / are / has* | actions hidden in nouns or missing (Ch 6) |
| You can't fill a gap by inference | the gap is in the *science*, not just the prose (Ch 5, Ch 7) |
| Blaming yourself for not understanding | the fault is usually the author's structure (Ch 4) |

## Order-of-operations for a hard passage
1. Subject-verb gaps → close them.
2. Topic positions → old info / consistent story-owner.
3. Stress positions → the new point of each sentence.
4. Verbs → make them carry the action.
5. Re-read: gaps that now surface → add the missing connections.
6. Apply principles as **principles, not rules** — violate only deliberately.

## Remember
- Structure *is* meaning: same facts, different structure → different interpretation.
- Improving the writing improves the thinking — revision exposes conceptual gaps.
- You can't make a sentence mean one thing; you can raise the odds most readers read it as you intend.
