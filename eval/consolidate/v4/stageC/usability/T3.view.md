### C-STEPDEN

*Answer, as the research gave it; not checked by a second reader.* **Verdict**

**PARTIALLY DEMONSTRATED.** Constraint-based/FBA models *have* represented N-oxide reduction as discrete enzymatic reactions with N₂O as an explicit, distinct modelled output — but only in the nitrifier-denitrification branch of ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014, 2016; Mellbye et al. 2018), where N₂O is terminal because the organism lacks *nosZ* and emission is mechanistically framed as electron-equivalent overflow under O₂ limitation. The specific full target — a genome-scale or community FBA model resolving NO₃⁻→NO₂⁻→NO→N₂O→N₂ as four separate fluxes (nar/nap, nirK/nirS, norBC, nosZ) with N₂O emerging as a truncation/accumulation product due to a missing/regulated *nosZ* or community pathway gap — was NOT found in the constraint-based literature; that capability currently resides exclusively in the kinetic/ODE, cybernetic, and trait-based ecosystem modelling families.

### the four-step denitrification pathway

- The denitrification pathway is a four-step dissimilatory reduction, NO₃⁻→NO₂⁻→NO→N₂O→N₂, catalysed by nitrate reductase (Nar/Nap), nitrite reductase (NirK/NirS), nitric oxide reductase (cNor/qNor, norBC) and nitrous oxide reductase (NosZ). (the note cites no source for it)
  Holds for: the full canonical heterotrophic chain, as the review describes it; no organism named.
- The four-step reduction consumes 10 electrons per two nitrate ions converted to dinitrogen. (the note cites no source for it)
  Holds for: the full pathway from nitrate to dinitrogen.

### constraint-based (FBA) models and the kinetic boundary

Builds on: the four-step denitrification pathway.

- Flux balance analysis (FBA) is a mathematical approach for analysing the flow of metabolites through a metabolic network: the network's stoichiometry, as mass balances at steady state, constrains the fluxes, and linear programming finds the flux distribution that optimises a stated biological objective, such as biomass production when growth is predicted. (Orth et al. 2010)
  Background, not from the research.
- In constraint-based modelling, the four denitrification reactions can in principle be encoded as separate fluxes balanced against an electron donor pool (NADH/quinone) with proton translocation. (the reviewer's synthesis)
  Holds for: stated as possible in principle, for the pathway NO₃⁻→NO₂⁻→NO→N₂O→N₂.
- The boundary between "constraint-based" and "kinetic" models is occasionally blurred in the literature (for example dynamic FBA and cybernetic models); the review classified each model by whether its core engine is stoichiometric LP optimization (FBA family) or rate-equation integration (kinetic/ODE family). (the reviewer's method statement)
  Holds for: the classification used throughout the review.

### kinetic/ODE, cybernetic and trait-based models of denitrification

Builds on: the four-step denitrification pathway, constraint-based (FBA) models and the kinetic boundary.

- Stepwise denitrification with N₂O as an explicit intermediate or terminal product is resolved in the kinetic/ODE and trait-based modelling families (the Activated Sludge Model for Nitrogen, electron-competition models, the cybernetic models of Song & Liu, the Kampschreur Agrobacterium model, and the PNAS/bioRxiv OMZ modular-denitrification ecosystem model); these are not constraint-based FBA models. (Kampschreur et al. 2012; Song and Liu 2015; Song et al. 2017; Hiatt and Grady 2008; Zakem et al. 2024; Zakem et al. 2024; Pan and others n.d.)
  Holds for: kinetic/ODE, cybernetic and trait-based families, not FBA.
  Evidence: rated "well established" by the research.
- The Activated Sludge Model for Nitrogen (ASMN; Hiatt & Grady, 2008) "incorporates four-step denitrification (sequential reduction of nitrate to nitrogen gas via nitrite, nitric oxide, and nitrous oxide) using individual, reaction-specific parameters." (Hiatt and Grady 2008)
  Holds for: an activated sludge process model (Water Environment Research 80(11):2145–2156), outside the FBA/constraint-based family.
- Electron-competition models (Pan et al., ES&T) explicitly model competition among the four denitrification steps for electrons to predict N₂O accumulation. (Pan and others n.d.)
  Holds for: kinetic models, outside the FBA/constraint-based family.

  Holds for: cybernetic dynamic metabolic models of denitrifying bacterial growth, outside the FBA/constraint-based family.
- The Kampschreur et al. (2012) Agrobacterium tumefaciens model resolves Nap/NirK/NorB with transcriptional/kinetic regulation and reproduces NO/N₂O emission from incomplete pathway expression; it is a kinetic ODE network, not FBA. (Kampschreur et al. 2012)
  Holds for: Agrobacterium tumefaciens; a kinetic ODE metabolic network.
  Evidence: modelled.

### constraint-based models of nitrifier denitrification

Builds on: constraint-based (FBA) models and the kinetic boundary.

- Perez-Garcia et al. (2014) built a stoichiometric metabolic network (SMN) of Nitrosomonas europaea energy metabolism (51 reactions, 44 metabolites) and used FBA to quantify NO and N₂O production through two distinct routes, the hydroxylamine-oxidation pathway and nitrifier denitrification (NO₂⁻→NO→N₂O via NirK and NorB). (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: N. europaea energy metabolism, an ammonia-oxidizing bacterium.
  Evidence: modelled.
- In the Perez-Garcia SMN, N₂O is an explicit output and, because N. europaea lacks nosZ, necessarily a terminal product. (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: N. europaea, which has no nosZ.
- The Perez-Garcia SMN is the clearest case where FBA quantifies NO and N₂O as distinct products through resolved enzymatic reactions (NirK, NorB, cytochrome P460, cytochrome aa3, HAO). (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: N. europaea SMN (51 reactions, 44 metabolites).
- The Perez-Garcia SMN was validated against measured yield ratios: "These estimates matched well, deviating on average by 15% from values for 17 M yield ratios reported for non-limiting oxygen and ammonium concentrations." (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: 17 M yield ratios under non-limiting oxygen and ammonium concentrations; N. europaea.
  Evidence: modelled and measured.
- Perez-Garcia et al. (2016, Biotechnology and Bioengineering) extended this lineage to a multispecies metabolic network that "captures the nitrogen respiratory pathways inferred from genomes of eight AOB and NOB species," resolving specific NO redox reactions (NirK, CytP460, cytochrome c554, flavohemoglobins, cNor). (Perez-Garcia et al. 2016)
  Holds for: eight AOB and NOB species, pathways inferred from genomes.
- In the 2016 multispecies network, "Simulations showed that NO oxidation to NO2(-) decreased N2O formation by 60% without changing culture's NO2(-) production rate." (Perez-Garcia et al. 2016)
  Holds for: simulations of the eight-species AOB/NOB metabolic network during nitrification.
  Evidence: modelled.
- The review's model index gives the 2016 multispecies network's N₂O mechanism as "NO redox balance; CytP460 reduces N2O by 60%", with N₂O explicit and sub-steps partly resolved (NO redox reactions, NirK, CytP460, cNor). (Perez-Garcia et al. 2016)
  Holds for: eight AOB/NOB species; stoichiometric network with FBA.
- The Mellbye et al. (2018) coculture model tracks NO, NO₂ and N₂O as separate N-oxide species, and is the closest community-level and strongest community-scale constraint-based demonstration of explicit N₂O output. (Mellbye et al. 2018)
  Holds for: a coculture of N. europaea and N. winogradskyi.

  Holds for: N. europaea and N. winogradskyi coculture.

(Mellbye et al. 2018)
  Holds for: N. winogradskyi, whose genome has no known NOR-encoding gene.

  Holds for: the N. europaea and N. winogradskyi coculture.

### N2O emission as electron overflow

Builds on: constraint-based models of nitrifier denitrification.

- Perez-Garcia et al. (2014) found that "NO and N2O are emitted when the sum of their production rates is greater than half the rate of NO oxidation by cytochrome P460." (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: the N. europaea SMN.
  Evidence: modelled.
- Perez-Garcia et al. (2014) found that "N2O is produced due to electron flow imbalances in the cell." (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: the N. europaea SMN.
  Evidence: modelled.
- Perez-Garcia et al. (2014) found that "Cytochromes play a key role in N2O production by directing electron flow." (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: the N. europaea SMN.
  Evidence: modelled.
- Perez-Garcia et al. (2014) found that "The cytochrome P460 reaction can potentially mitigate N2O emissions." (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: the N. europaea SMN.
  Evidence: modelled.
- The Perez-Garcia (2014) model's central, validated finding is that NO/N₂O emission is an electron-overflow phenomenon: nitrifier denitrification is "activated when the availability of electron donors exceeds that of electron sinks," dissipating excess reducing equivalents when O₂ is limiting. (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014)
  Holds for: N. europaea under O₂ limitation.
- Both Perez-Garcia et al. (2014) and Mellbye et al. (2018) attribute N₂O emission to an intracellular electron-equivalent imbalance under O₂ limitation, not to a missing nosZ or a pathway gap in a four-step chain. (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014; Mellbye et al. 2018)
  Holds for: the two stoichiometric/FBA models of nitrifier denitrification.
- In the demonstrated nitrifier-denitrification models, each transformation is encoded as a distinct reaction tied to its enzyme (NO₂⁻→NO by NirK; NO→N₂O by NorB), with N-oxides as intracellular species that can be drained by exchange reactions. (the reviewer's synthesis of the nitrifier-denitrification models)
  Holds for: the nitrifier-denitrification cases the review calls demonstrated.
- In these models the electron-balance logic is what forces emission: N₂O/NO appear when reducing-equivalent production outruns the available electron sink (O₂). (the reviewer's synthesis of the nitrifier-denitrification models)
  Holds for: the nitrifier-denitrification models.
- The constraint-based representation works for nitrifier denitrification because the model treats N-oxide release as an electron-overflow valve rather than as the controlled, regulated sequential respiration of a canonical denitrifier. (the reviewer's synthesis)
  Holds for: nitrifier denitrification, contrasted with canonical denitrifiers.
- Constraint-based/FBA models that explicitly output N₂O do so only where N₂O is a forced electron-overflow terminus in an organism lacking nosZ (nitrifier denitrification in N. europaea); their N₂O is nitrifier-denitrification-derived and electron-overflow-driven, not a four-step heterotrophic truncation. (Perez-Garcia et al. 2014; Mellbye et al. 2018)
  Holds for: Perez-Garcia et al. (2014) and Mellbye et al. (2018); N. europaea.
