*Answer, as the research gave it; not checked by a second reader.* **TL;DR**

- All four questions are at best PARTIALLY demonstrated and at worst genuinely open: the published feature-propagation robustness results (Rossi et al., LoG 2022) cover homophilous, MCAR, mostly-sparse-feature, small-to-moderate-missing-fraction regimes, none of which match biblion's ~70%-featureless, dense-SPECTER2-feature, structural-missingness regime. - The single most important finding: the mechanism biblion most needs to fear is documented. Diffusion-based imputation provably produces low-variance, near-constant channels under high missingness (Um et al., ICML 2025), and zero-imputation biases representation magnitude in proportion to the observed fraction (Yi et al., ICLR 2020, the "variable sparsity problem"). (These are the failure modes of recovering structure under missing high-dimensional data in the graph/diffusion regime.) Both directly threaten the co-citation bridge (Q1) and motivate a conduit-only operator (Q3). - biblion should not assume imputed ghost anchors are reliable. The defensible path is to raise the degree threshold to shrink the featureless fraction (Q2) AND to test a masked/no-self-anchor APPNP variant that keeps ghosts in the propagation topology but excludes their fabricated anchor from the teleport term (Q3), while measuring whether the PCA denoise stage amplifies or dampens ghost distortion (Q4).

*Answer, as the research gave it; not checked by a second reader.* **Key Findings**

1. **The "proven zone" for feature propagation does not include biblion's regime.** Rossi et al. report only a 4.12% relative accuracy drop at 99% missing features, but explicitly under MCAR per-channel masking on homophilous benchmarks, and they note the method degrades toward the zero baseline as homophily falls. biblion has structural (whole-node) missingness, dense features, and ~70% featureless nodes at degree >= 2. 2. **The co-citation bridge is encoded by construction but not validated downstream.** Personalized-PageRank propagation (APPNP) does transmit similarity through shared intermediaries, and second-order proximity theory (LINE) confirms shared-neighbor nodes should embed closely. But a fabricated/zero anchor on the bridge node injects a low-variance or origin-pulling signal that variance-collapse and oversmoothing can wash out before UMAP/HDBSCAN ever see it. 3. **A degree-threshold crossover exists in principle but is dataset-specific.** Rossi et al. observe methods perform well up to ~50% missing and that "the gap between methods opens up from around 60% of missing features." This ~60% figure is a downstream-accuracy crossover on sparse benchmarks, not a guarantee for dense SPECTER2 geometry, and raising the threshold discards graph structure. 4. **Conduit-only (masked) participation is a recognized and defensible operator design.** Virtual-node and masked-aggregation literature supports keeping a node in the propagation topology while excluding its (absent) features from the aggregation term. No paper tests exactly biblion's zero-anchor-corruption mechanism, so this is a design recommendation, not a proven result. 5. **The denoise-then-propagate vs propagate-then-denoise interaction under high dense missingness is essentially unstudied.** The pieces exist (PCA-a-priori experiments on full features; zero-imputation magnitude bias; SVD-spectrum bias under zero-imputation) but the specific combination biblion runs is an open question.

### zero-imputation and the origin pull

Builds on: APPNP (personalized-PageRank) propagation, featureless ("ghost") nodes in biblion's citation graph.

- Yi et al. (ICLR 2020) prove the variable sparsity problem (VSP): with zero-imputation "the output of a predictive model largely varies with respect to the rate of missingness in the given input," scaling with the observed fraction, so that zero-imputation biases representation magnitude in proportion to the observed fraction; they correct it with Sparsity Normalization. (Yi et al. 2020)
  Holds for: non-graph models, MCAR missingness.
  Evidence: derived.
- Yi et al. prove that output magnitude scales with the observed fraction (their Theorem 1: expected output proportional to mu_m). (Yi et al. 2020)
  Holds for: non-graph models, MCAR missingness.
  Evidence: derived.
- Yi et al. note that GCN's Laplacian normalization "precisely matches" their Sparsity Normalization operation, tying the mechanism directly to graph message passing. (Yi et al. 2020)
  Holds for: GCN Laplacian normalization.
- A zero-anchored ghost injects zeros into its neighbours' diffusion sums; in an averaging operator like D^-1 A it does what Rossi et al. describe for the Zero baseline ("the effect of zero-imputation is simply to reduce the norm of the average embeddings"), so it pulls its real neighbours toward the origin, distorting real-node geometry. (Rossi et al. 2022)
  Holds for: an averaging operator such as D^-1 A with zero-anchored ghosts.
  Evidence: rated "well supported" by the research.

### the PCA denoise stage and ghost injection

Builds on: zero-imputation and the origin pull, sparse against dense, uniform against structural missingness.

- The interaction of the PCA denoise stage with ghost injection (PCA denoise of real anchors x ghost injection, and denoise-then-propagate vs propagate-then-denoise) is unstudied at biblion's missingness and feature density: the component results exist (PCA-a-priori experiments on full features; zero-imputation magnitude bias; SVD-spectrum bias under zero-imputation) but no single source tests their interaction. (the review's verdict on the question)
  Holds for: high, dense missingness (~70% missing, dense features).
  Evidence: a search result; rated "NO DIRECT EVIDENCE; genuinely open" by the research.
  The research does not say whether this was searched for.
- Morehead et al. (arXiv:2203.12522) apply PCA-100 and an autoencoder a priori (before the GNN) and find it preserves accuracy and reduces prediction variance across seeds (Cora GCN: 80.82% original, std 0.62, vs 80.98% PCA-100, std 0.80, means over five seeds), though one model (GraphConv) degraded badly with the autoencoder bottleneck. (Morehead et al. 2022)
  Holds for: full features with no missingness, sparse bag-of-words (Cora); reduce-then-propagate on complete data; not shown for ghosts.
- In Morehead et al., one model (GraphConv) degraded badly with the autoencoder bottleneck. (Morehead et al. 2022)
  Holds for: full features, no missingness.
- In classical PCA-with-missing-data, treating missing entries as zero biases the covariance/singular-value spectrum downward, and regularized iterative PCA (missMDA) deliberately shrinks singular values further as missingness grows. (Josse and Husson 2016; Anonymous n.d.; Anonymous n.d.)
  Holds for: non-graph PCA with missing data.
- "Impute-then-SVD" and "SVD-on-observed-only" (O-ALS vs I-SVD) are distinct procedures that introduce different biases: the non-graph analogue of biblion's ordering question. (Anonymous n.d.)
  Holds for: non-graph PCA with missing data.
- Any preprocessing that rescales magnitude (PCA centering, whitening, normalization) interacts with the zero-imputation magnitude bias. (Yi et al. 2020)
  Holds for: preprocessing before propagation.
- In biblion the PCA denoise runs on the real anchors only (a ghost has no features to denoise), so denoising happens before ghosts get any anchor; PCA is fit on real nodes only, so the basis is defined by real-feature covariance. (the note's description of biblion)
  Holds for: the biblion pipeline.
- Denoising tends to dampen the raw magnitude of low-variance directions. (the reviewer's reasoning)
  Holds for: PCA denoising.
- If a ghost is zero-anchored after denoising, the origin-pull it exerts is on already-centered, reduced-variance real vectors; the relative distortion could be larger (each retained dimension carries more relative weight) or smaller (absolute magnitudes are smaller), and the literature does not resolve which dominates at ~70% missing on dense features. (the reviewer's reasoning)
  Holds for: ~70% missing, dense features.
- The denoise-then-propagate order biblion uses is plausibly more robust than propagate-then-denoise, because FP-style diffusion is itself a low-pass denoiser, so denoising first avoids double-smoothing the imputed values. (the reviewer's inference)
  Holds for: biblion's denoise-then-propagate order.
  Evidence: the reviewer's reading.

  Holds for: the sources the review located for the denoise question.

  Holds for: PCA at 100-d.
