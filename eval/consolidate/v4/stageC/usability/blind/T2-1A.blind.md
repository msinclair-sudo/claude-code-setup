Errors (quote each sentence):
- "A zero-anchored ghost might even push energy back into directions PCA removed." Diffusion runs entirely in the retained 100-dimensional space and is linear per dimension. A zero anchor in that space (the mean, if the data are centred) cannot create components in the discarded directions. The hedge doesn't rescue a mechanism that can't happen. Minor.
- "Yi and colleagues (ICLR 2020) proved the general form: with zero-filling, a model's output scales with the fraction of the input actually observed." Yi et al. analysed ordinary (non-graph) neural networks with zero-imputed inputs. Calling it "the general form" and using it as proof that ghosts distort graph neighbours ("So the ghost distorts papers whose own features are perfectly good") stretches it. Minor (overstated scope).
- "Features were sparse word-count vectors." Not all of them. Feature Propagation's benchmarks included OGBN-Arxiv, whose features are dense averaged word embeddings, and PubMed uses TF-IDF rather than counts. Minor overgeneralisation.
- "Um and colleagues (ICML 2025) proved that diffusion-based imputation produces channels whose values are nearly identical across nodes, and that such low-variance channels contribute very little downstream." Drops the heavy-missingness condition, and "proved" for the downstream-contribution part is probably firmer than the paper's mix of theory and experiment supports. Minor (overstatement).
- Imprecision, not counted: "every node replaces its vector with a mix: 70% the average of its neighbours' current vectors". APPNP propagates with the symmetrically normalised adjacency (with self-loops), not a plain mean. This is a reasonable simplification for a lay reader.

Notes:
- The prose is strong and the "ghost" and "conduit" framing works. The weaknesses are epistemic: absences are stated as facts, inferences read like findings ("The bridge is encoded; nobody has shown it survives" is fair, but "The 50 to 60% figure is an optimistic ceiling" is not hedged), and nothing marks where the evidence comes from.
- Comparing a 70% share of featureless nodes with a 50 to 60% share of missing entries is a cross-unit comparison. The piece half-acknowledges this ("under conditions that should pull the crossover lower") but still states the conclusion flatly.
- Yun and colleagues are cited without a year or venue.
- The closing measurement plan (variance of ghost vectors, a rewired-ghost control, a threshold sweep) is the most actionable part.
