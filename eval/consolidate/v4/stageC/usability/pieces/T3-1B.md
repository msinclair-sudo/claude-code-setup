# Where the nitrous oxide goes: denitrification in flux balance models

Denitrification takes nitrate apart one step at a time. Nitrate (NO₃⁻) is reduced to nitrite (NO₂⁻), nitrite to nitric oxide (NO), nitric oxide to nitrous oxide (N₂O), and nitrous oxide to dinitrogen gas (N₂). Each step has its own enzyme: nitrate reductase (Nar or Nap), nitrite reductase (NirK or NirS), nitric oxide reductase (Nor, encoded by *norBC*) and nitrous oxide reductase (NosZ). Carrying two nitrate ions all the way to one N₂ consumes ten electrons. Not every microbe has the whole chain; many carry only a subset of it, a "module" (Zakem et al. 2024). A model that is to say anything about N₂O has to keep the steps apart.

Constraint-based models can do this in principle. In practice, the ones that report N₂O come from a corner of microbiology you might not look in first.

## How a flux balance model holds a pathway

Flux balance analysis (FBA) represents metabolism as a network of reactions with fixed stoichiometry. At steady state every internal metabolite is made as fast as it is used, which bounds the possible fluxes. Linear programming then picks the flux distribution that best serves a stated objective, often biomass production (Orth et al. 2010). There are no rate constants: the model says which flows are feasible, not how fast they run.

The four denitrification steps fit this frame. Each becomes its own reaction, drawing electrons from a shared donor pool (NADH, quinones) and coupled to proton translocation. Give an intermediate such as N₂O an exchange reaction, a route out of the cell, and its outflow becomes a model output like any other.

The difficulty is the objective. A model that maximises biomass does not, unaided, predict that a cell will release a partly reduced byproduct. It needs extra constraints, a limitation known across work on byproduct secretion generally.

## Where FBA does report N₂O

The clear cases come from ammonia-oxidising bacteria, which are nitrifiers rather than classic denitrifiers. *Nitrosomonas europaea* can reduce nitrite to NO and NO to N₂O, a route called nitrifier denitrification. It has no *nosZ*, so N₂O is the end of its line.

Perez-Garcia et al. (2014) built a stoichiometric network of *N. europaea*'s energy metabolism (51 reactions, 44 metabolites) and used FBA to quantify NO and N₂O production through two routes: hydroxylamine oxidation, and nitrifier denitrification via NirK and NorB. Each transformation is a separate reaction tied to its enzyme, and N₂O is an explicit output. Its estimates came within 15%, on average, of 17 published yield ratios measured with oxygen and ammonium plentiful.

What makes N₂O appear in this model is electron balance. Nitrifier denitrification is "activated when the availability of electron donors exceeds that of electron sinks", which happens when oxygen runs short; NO and N₂O carry off the surplus reducing power. The authors found that cytochromes steer N₂O production by directing electron flow, and that NO and N₂O are emitted once their combined production rate exceeds half the rate at which cytochrome P460 oxidises NO. P460 could therefore damp emissions. The model works because it treats N-oxide release as an overflow valve, not as the regulated, sequential respiration of a true denitrifier.

The same group widened this to a network of the nitrogen respiratory pathways of eight ammonia- and nitrite-oxidising species (Perez-Garcia et al. 2016). In simulations, oxidising NO back to nitrite cut N₂O formation by 60% without changing nitrite production. (One account credits the 60% to cytochrome P460, the NO-oxidising enzyme in the 2014 model; the two descriptions may be the same finding.)

Mellbye et al. (2018) moved to genome scale and to a community: *N. europaea* grown with the nitrite oxidiser *Nitrobacter winogradskyi*. They joined two genome-scale reconstructions (iFC578 and iFC579), added abiotic NO-oxidation reactions in the gas and liquid phases, and ran the result as dynamic FBA. NO, NO₂ and N₂O are tracked as separate species. Higher emissions from the pair are put down to a microaerobic environment: the nitrite oxidiser adds to the oxygen demand of ammonia oxidation. Gene content decides who can emit: *N. winogradskyi* has no known NOR gene, so its small observed N₂O production is left out. This is the only community-scale constraint-based model found that outputs N₂O as a distinct flux.

Two cautions. How each N-oxide reaction was balanced for mass and charge could not be checked for the 2014 or 2018 models, whose full methods were only partly accessible, and such balancing is error-prone. And the lineage did not carry N₂O forward: the re-curated 2022 *N. europaea* reconstruction, iGC535, predicts growth and oxygen uptake with about 90% accuracy but does not resolve the N-oxide steps or report N₂O (Canto-Encalada et al. 2022).

## Denitrifiers proper

Genome-scale models exist for several complete denitrifiers: *Paracoccus denitrificans* Pd1222 (Bordel et al. 2024), *Thauera* sp. MZ1T (iThauera861; Tec-Campos et al. 2025), *Pseudomonas stutzeri* A1501 (iPB890, iQY1018; Babaei et al. 2015; Yuan et al. 2023) and *P. aeruginosa* (iMO1056, iSD1509; Oberhardt et al. 2008; Dahal et al. 2023). The *Paracoccus* and *Thauera* models carry genes for all four steps. None was built or tested to report N₂O. The *Paracoccus* model was validated on biomass yields, including with nitrate as electron acceptor; the *Thauera* model was put to work on polymers and pollutant breakdown, though the paper notes the organism makes NO and N₂O; the *Pseudomonas* models treat denitrification as a route to N₂ and energy. Automated draft reconstructions of *P. stutzeri* built with the CarveMe tool lack a working anaerobic nitrate-respiration chain altogether.

Community models report aggregate capability and the fluxes exchanged between members, not resolved sub-steps; gut reconstructions such as AGORA include nitrate and nitrite reductases but give no stepwise readout.

So the specific model one might want — a genome-scale or community FBA model with all four steps as separate fluxes, where N₂O accumulates because NosZ is missing or limited, or because the community splits the pathway — was not found in the published constraint-based literature as of June 2026. The search was finite; a recent or unpublished model could exist. Building one would mean adding exchange reactions for each intermediate, explicit electron coupling for each reductase, and constraints such as a cap on NosZ flux to mimic copper limitation or regulatory lag. The *Paracoccus* and *Thauera* models are the obvious starting points.

## Kinetic and trait-based models

Kinetic models, which integrate a rate equation for each reaction over time, already resolve the steps. The Activated Sludge Model for Nitrogen (Hiatt and Grady 2008) handles four-step denitrification with separate parameters for each reaction. Electron-competition models (Pan et al.) have the four steps compete for a limited electron supply, which predicts when N₂O builds up. Kampschreur et al. (2012) modelled *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* with Nap, NirK and NorB under transcriptional and kinetic regulation; incomplete, unbalanced enzyme expression during the switch from oxic to anoxic conditions reproduces its NO and N₂O emissions. Cybernetic models (Song and Liu 2015; Song et al. 2017) resolve the sequential intermediates with regulation that changes over time. Dynamic FBA and cybernetic models blur the line with FBA; the sorting here is by core engine, optimisation versus rate equations.

Trait-based models come at it from ecology. Ocean biogeochemical models have traditionally collapsed denitrification into one step. Zakem et al. (2024) instead model the pathway as modules (nitrate to nitrite to N₂O to N₂, leaving out NO), with organisms defined by redox thermodynamics, biomass yield and competition for resources. N₂O cycling then emerges from niche partitioning: one functional type reduces nitrate to nitrite, another N₂O to N₂. This is exactly the community pathway gap no published FBA model has been found to capture, and it is done without a stoichiometric network at all.

The comparison turns on mechanism. Where FBA reports N₂O, it is an overflow product in an organism that cannot reduce it further. Where kinetic and trait-based models report it, N₂O is a pathway cut short by regulation, competition for electrons, or a division of labour between microbes.
