# Marking scheme: stage C usability pieces

## How to use it

- Grade each piece on its own first, before comparing it with its partner. Don't open the key, the blind readers' verdicts or the fact-checks until all 12 pieces are graded.
- Score each criterion 0–3 using the anchors. If a piece falls between two anchors, choose the lower score.
- Log every specific error you find, quoting the sentence. These get checked against the knowledge note afterwards.
- Part B is recorded but not scored. Those criteria belong to the writing skill, which isn't built yet.

## Part A: scored (these reflect the input the writer was given)

| # | Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Accuracy.** Nothing wrong or overstated, judged with your own knowledge of the field. | Several errors, or one serious error | Two or more minor errors | One minor error | No errors found |
| A2 | **Calibration.** Each claim's strength matches its evidence: single studies stay single, settings stay attached, hedges are kept. | Generalises freely | Often firmer than the evidence | Mostly matched; an occasional overreach | Strength matches evidence throughout |
| A3 | **Access-appropriate phrasing.** Abstract-only or second-hand findings are reported as what the authors state, without added interpretation. Full-text findings can be summarised and interpreted. | Interprets abstract-only findings boldly | Access is ignored | Mostly respected | Phrasing consistently matches access |
| A4 | **Absences qualified.** "Untested", "unknown" or "no evidence" says whether the research searched for it and didn't find it, or didn't search. | Absences stated as facts about the literature | Mostly unqualified | Mostly qualified | Every absence is qualified |
| A5 | **Opposing evidence.** Contrary findings appear where they exist, and conclusions say whether opposing positions were searched for. | One-sided | Contrary evidence is mentioned but doesn't affect the conclusions | Contrary evidence is included and weighed | Included, weighed, and its search status given |
| A6 | **Scope and breadth.** The piece tells the reader how far its conclusions can be taken (one review, which settings, what wasn't covered). | No scope given | Vague | Stated for the main conclusions | Clear throughout |
| A7 | **Attribution.** Findings are tied to named studies in the text. | None | Occasional | Most key findings | Consistently |
| A8 | **Depth and coverage.** The important points of the topic are present, with enough depth to matter. | Surface skim | Thin, or key points missing | Solid, with minor gaps | Rich, nothing important missing |
| A9 | **Stands alone.** A reader can follow it without the research; it has a coherent arc. | Hard to follow | Readable but disjointed | Coherent | Coherent and well shaped |
| A10 | **Usefulness.** Would you build a video or presentation from it? | No | Only with major rework | With moderate rework | Yes, as a starting point |

**Part A total: / 30**

## Part B: recorded, not scored (writing-skill criteria for later)

Mark each one **Yes / Partly / No**, with a note if useful.

- B1. Reference list present and complete
- B2. Dense numerical comparisons put in a table or figure
- B3. Cognitive load: too many facts at once?
- B4. Prose quality and style
- B5. Ambiguity flagged as ambiguity

## After grading

- Unseal the key and compare your scores across conditions. Is the difference consistent across topics and across the two writers?
- Compare your scores with the blind readers' scores on the same criteria. Where you disagree most, read the two pieces again.
