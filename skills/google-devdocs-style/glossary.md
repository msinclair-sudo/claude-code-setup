# Glossary

Terms defined by the Google developer documentation style guide, with the chapter that covers each.
For rulings on *specific words* (which term to use, which to avoid), see [word-list.md](word-list.md).

**Ableist language** — Words like *crazy*, *insane*, *blind to*, *cripple*, *dumb* used figuratively; replace with a term that names the actual meaning. (Ch 8)

**Acronym** — An abbreviation formed from initial letters and pronounced as a word (*NATO*, *scuba*). Commonly used to cover initialisms too. (Ch 4)

**Anthropomorphism** — Attributing human qualities to software or hardware; a form of figurative language. (Ch 1)

**Antecedent** — The noun a pronoun replaces. (Ch 1)

**Bare infinitive** — The plain base form of a verb; the correct opening for a task-based heading. Also called plain form or base form. (Ch 2)

**Click-to-copy** — A code block the reader copies whole; must contain nothing requiring deletion. (Ch 6)

**Cognitive load** — The decision cost each link imposes on a reader. (Ch 9)

**Coordinating conjunction** — *and*, *but*, *or*, *nor*, *for*, *so*, *yet*. (Ch 3)

**Cross-reference** — A link to supplementary information not required for the current task. (Ch 9)

**Custom anchor** — A hand-authored `id` on a heading that keeps inbound links working when heading text changes. (Ch 2)

**Description list** — A list of terms each paired with a definition (`dl`/`dt`/`dd`). (Ch 2)

**Document comment** — A source-code comment from which API reference documentation is generated. (Ch 6)

**Em dash (—)** — Marks a break or interruption; no surrounding spaces; never spelled with hyphens. (Ch 3)

**Excessive claim** — An assertion about performance, cost, or security that the reader can't verify, that an incident would falsify, or that disparages a third party. (Ch 1)

**Figurative language** — Metaphor and idiom; imprecise, hard to translate, and often exclusionary. (Ch 8)

**Image map** — A coordinate-overlay hotspot region; avoided for accessibility and reliability reasons. (Ch 10)

**Initialism** — An abbreviation formed from initial letters, pronounced letter by letter (*CIA*, *FYI*). (Ch 4)

**Internationalization** — Designing a product and its docs to minimize later localization effort. (Ch 8)

**Jargon** — Specialized, often figurative terminology meaningful mainly within a specific group; includes vague overloaded terms like *solution* or *workload*. (Ch 1)

**Localization** — Adapting a product and its documentation for a specific country, including currency and units. (Ch 8)

**NANP** — North American Numbering Plan; governs the area-code phone format. (Ch 7)

**Nonbreaking hyphen (`&#8209;`)** — Keeps a hyphenated string such as a phone number on one line. (Ch 7)

**Nonbreaking space (`&nbsp;`)** — Keeps a number and its unit on the same line. (Ch 7)

**Notice** — Offset content (Note, Caution, Warning) carrying information outside the main flow. (Ch 5)

**PII** — Personally identifiable information: names, email addresses, domains, phone numbers, project names, credit card numbers. (Ch 10, Ch 11)

**Placeholder** — A named stand-in for a value the reader supplies or that varies, such as `PROJECT_ID`. (Ch 6)

**Prescriptive (opinionated) documentation** — Documentation that recommends a path rather than listing options. (Ch 12)

**Punycode** — ASCII encoding for internationalized (non-ASCII) hostnames. (Ch 11)

**Reference hierarchy** — The fixed order for resolving style questions: project style → this guide → third-party references. (Ch 2)

**Run-in heading** — A bolded lead term inside a list item, followed by its explanation. (Ch 2, Ch 5)

**Semantic tagging** — Marking content by meaning rather than appearance; unavailable in Markdown. (Ch 5)

**Sentence case** — Capitalizing only the first word and proper nouns; required for titles and headings. (Ch 4)

**Serial (Oxford) comma** — The comma before the final conjunction in a series of three or more; mandatory. (Ch 3)

**Shortened word** — Part of a word or phrase, sometimes with a trailing period (*Dr.*, *etc.*, *min*). (Ch 4)

**Single-step procedure** — A one-action task, formatted as a bullet rather than a one-item numbered list. (Ch 2)

**Suspension points** — Ellipses indicating hesitation; not used in Google documentation. (Ch 3)

**Timeless documentation** — Documentation free of temporal anchors and version-comparison framing. (Ch 12)

**Title case (init-capped)** — Every word capitalized except prepositions and articles; Google's default for product names. (Ch 11)

**Translation** — Converting one language into another; may involve localization but isn't synonymous with it. (Ch 8)

**Unicase** — A writing system with no upper/lower case distinction. (Ch 8)

**Words as words** — Referring to a word, phrase, or letter as itself; takes italics. (Ch 5)
