# Notes on writing piece.md

Length: the body runs to about 1,310 words including title and headings (about 1,285 of prose), a little over the 1,200 target. A short "Sources named" list follows; it is not counted.

## 1. What I wanted or needed and could not find in the material

- **Any study that answers the main question directly.** No held-out comparison of measured metagenomic gene profiles against taxonomic profiles from the same samples. The piece has to say "not done" at its centre, and the material gives no way to tell whether that reflects the literature or the search routes, which were badly degraded (web search exhausted, two scholarly APIs rate-limited).
- **Averill et al. 2021, main text.** Never obtained. I could not say how the authors arrived at "equally predictable" or "fungal predictability rises with taxonomic scale", or what their Fig. 4 shows per rank. Extended Data Fig. 1 (a within-NEON 70/30 cross-validation of functional against taxonomic groups) is flagged by the critic as relevant, but no numbers are given, so I could not use it.
- **Werbin et al. 2024, the numbers behind "functional groups more predictable than most taxonomic groups".** Only the qualitative statement is available. New-site transfer is split by kingdom, not by profile type, so I could not say whether functional groups transferred better or worse than taxa at new sites. That is the comparison the piece most needed from this study.
- **Werbin's CRPS figures.** It is unclear whether "worse by 65% against 8%" is a relative change in mean CRPS, and the material does not define CRPS. I wrote "forecast error worsened by 65%" and hope that is right.
- **The two 2026 studies the critic added.** Aouabed et al.: abstract only, hold-out design unverified, and no numbers beyond fungal functional R² 0.45. The New Phytologist canopy-reflectance study: no authors or title, just a DOI and a range of R² 0.27 to 0.86. I could not say which resolution got which value, so I dropped the range.
- **Any held-out evidence on functional resolution** (gene against pathway against broad category). None. Chen et al. 2022 on redundancy is the only nearby item, and it is explanatory.
- **A sense of scale for a non-specialist.** Is a held-out R² of 0.28 good for this kind of problem? The material gives no benchmark, so readers have to take numbers like 0.26 and 0.28 without context.
- **Spatially blocked microbial hold-outs.** None reported, so the "random splits flatter the score" point leans on plant and animal benchmarks (Norberg 2019, plus Wenger and Olden 2012 from an abstract only).
- **Freshwater, sediment and marine time-series microbes**, and the plant and animal trait-based SDM transferability literature (Vesk 2021, Pollock 2012, fourth-corner and HMSC work). The critic names these as gaps without any content, so the piece cannot say whether traits transfer better than taxa in plants or animals at held-out sites.
- **Liu et al. 2023, definitions.** Whether "33 to 42% across taxonomic levels" is a range of means per level, and which levels, is not stated.
- **Statzner et al. 2005 numbers.** There are none, and whether the test was held out comes only from a secondary account, so I hedged with "reportedly".
- **What a derived-profile lookup is in practice.** FAPROTAX is named once and never explained. I described the method generically.

## 2. What in the material was hard to use

- **Layered, partly contradictory structure.** The note puts the critic's corrections first, then the "answers as delivered", then the body. Several body claims are overridden by the critic: the Averill "equally predictable" comparison should be read against high-level groups only; Van Sickle 2005 is calibration-site evidence, not held-out skill; "trait profiles barely vary, so a constant predicts them" is the reviewer's inference, and the Statzner abstract partly contradicts it; the freshwater trait advantage is rated too strongly. I had to reconcile each claim by hand against the critique before I could use it. A reader-facing version would need these merged.
- **Many incompatible skill measures**: best-fit R², 1:1 R², correlation, nRMSE, CRPS, Bray-Curtis similarity, AUC, SD of O/E. Almost no numbers can be compared across studies. I kept two (best-fit against 1:1 R²) and explained them. The rest I either dropped or reported with a "can't be ranked" warning.
- **Averill ranges that depend on handling of duplicate rows** (phylum 0.23 to 0.30), and two different "phylum" figures (0.27 from the critic, 0.23 to 0.30 in the table). I used the table's range for bacterial ranks overall.
- **Unexplained internal jargon**: D1/D2/D3, Cases A to D, evidence class, SINGLE SOURCE, ROUTING, preflight, O/E, Pc, LIRRs, MAGs, ASV/OTU, Longhurst province, nRMSE/CRPS. None of it is defined for an outside reader. I translated or dropped all of it.
- **Access labels on every claim** (full text, abstract only, secondary, via search agent, re-opened or not). These matter for weighing evidence, but there are too many gradations to carry into a short piece. I mentioned access only where it changes how far a claim can be trusted (the Aouabed abstract, the Werbin preprint, Statzner via a later review).
- **Usable-looking results that turned out unusable**: Hawkins et al. 2000, species beating family, is confounded with a threshold choice and not confirmed as held out. Van Sickle's SDs are calibration-site figures. Kruk 2011 is in-sample and compares morphological groups with coarse taxonomic groups. All three were left out.
- **Pipeline material with no use for the piece**: provenance and preflight notes, the Stage 2 routing check, the Crossref metadata audit, and the "Sources not obtained" list, which has several duplicate entries (Van Sickle 2005, Hawkins 2000, Clarke 2003 and others appear twice).
- **The explanatory freshwater evidence** combines three different measures (fit to environment, consistency across regions, low variability). The critic notes this and adds that the sources are abstract-only and come from one linked author network. That made it hard to state the "trait advantage" at any confidence other than weak.
